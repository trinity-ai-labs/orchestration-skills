# divergence

A **divergence** is an *unauthorized scope change* by a live agent: work that has moved outside the scope
the agent was dispatched with, which nobody holding that scope authorized. It is the condition a
dispatcher's mid-flight poll exists to detect, and the one thing its destructive lever fires on.

**Authorization is the whole of the definition, which is why divergence is a VERDICT rather than a property
of the diff.** Two agents can produce byte-identical hunks with one diverging and the other not; what
separates them is whether the party owning the scope granted that ground, and that fact lives in the
granting party's own record — a widened fence written onto the slice's PR, a brief, a boundary — never in
the tree. A diff is therefore evidence toward the verdict and never the verdict itself.

Three things follow. A **granted** widening is not an exception to divergence but not divergence at all —
a **grant** being one named path released to a slice by the party that owns its scope, written where it
outlives the run, which is the authorization the definition turns on. An agent **asking** for ground it does
not hold is not diverging while it asks, since asking is how a scope change becomes authorized, though the
ask authorizes nothing until it is answered. And a compile-driven **ripple** — the enum arms, config entries
or fixture lines an in-scope change forces — has not moved the scope at all, only the file count.

**A divergence is neither a stall nor a wrong brief.** A stalled agent has stopped producing work, where a
diverging one is producing work of the wrong kind. An agent working correctly to a brief that states a
wrong fact has not diverged either: its scope is intact and its premise is not, which makes that a
correction rather than a scope change.
